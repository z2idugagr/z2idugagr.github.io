AI Translation vs Human Translation: 7 Mistakes AI Still Makes in 2026

Hank Chen ・ November 16, 2026 ・ About 6 minutes reading

AI translation is fast, cheap, and good enough for understanding a foreign menu or a chat message. But the moment the text is public — a website, a contract summary, a product page, a subtitle file — the same seven mistakes keep showing up. They are not rare edge cases. They are the predictable failure modes of a system that guesses the most likely next word instead of understanding what the text is for.

Below are the seven mistakes, each with a real example in English ↔ Traditional Chinese (Taiwan). The "AI" versions are the kind of output machine translation actually produces; the "Human" versions are how a translator fixes them. If you already ran your text through AI, you don't need to start over — that is exactly what MTPE (machine translation post-editing) is for.

▍1. Literal idioms: words translated, meaning lost

Idioms are the first thing AI gets wrong, because the words and the meaning point in different directions.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"It's raining cats and dogs."正在下貓和狗。傾盆大雨。
"Break a leg!"摔斷一條腿！加油！祝你好運！

A reader who sees 下貓和狗 knows instantly that no human wrote it. Idioms need a translator who knows what the phrase does, not just what it says.

▍2. Wrong tone: formal where it should be friendly, stiff where it should sell

AI usually picks one register and keeps it. Marketing copy that should sound warm comes out like a government notice, and a friendly brand starts sounding like a legal department.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"Grab yours before they're gone!"請於售罄前取得您的商品。喜歡就快下手，賣完就沒了！
"We'd love to hear from you."我們很想聽到您的聲音。歡迎隨時與我們聯繫。

Tone is a business decision, not a grammar rule. A translator asks who the reader is before choosing the words.

▍3. Inconsistent terms: the same word, three different translations

AI translates sentence by sentence. In a 20-page document, the same term can come out three different ways — and the reader starts to wonder whether three different things are being discussed.

TermTypical AI output (same document)Human fix
"software"軟體、軟件、程式全篇統一用「軟體」
"settings"設定、設置、配置全篇統一用「設定」

This is worse than a typo: inconsistent terms make a document look unfinished. The human fix is boring and essential — a glossary made before translating, then used for every single page. For a ready-made starting point, see the free searchable glossary.

▍4. Mixed-up names: brands, products, and people translated by accident

AI cannot always tell a name from an ordinary word. Brand names get translated, product names get paraphrased, and the same person's name comes out differently on page 1 and page 10.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"Download the Apple app."下載蘋果應用程式。下載 Apple 應用程式。
"Contact the Foxconn team."聯絡富士康團隊。聯絡鴻海團隊。

In Taiwan, Foxconn is 鴻海， not a literal translation of "fox". Names are facts, not vocabulary — a human checks them against the client's own usage instead of guessing.

▍5. Missed cultural context: correct words, wrong message

Some words carry different meanings in different cultures, and AI has no way to know which culture the reader lives in.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"White is our wedding color."白色是我們的婚禮顏色。白色是我們的婚禮主色（西方婚禮象徵純潔；中文語境需注意白色另有喪事聯想）。
"The Year of the Dragon sale!"龍年特賣！龍年特賣！龍在華人文化象徵吉祥，行銷文案可直接用，不需改寫。

The words can be perfectly correct and the message still wrong. Cultural context is the one thing a machine cannot look up, because it is not written in the sentence — it is written in the reader.

▍6. Wrong formality: 你 vs 您， and who is talking to whom

Chinese marks respect with pronouns and word choice, and AI frequently gets the level wrong — too casual for a business letter, or stiffly formal for a social media post.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"Hi, thanks for reaching out!"嗨，感謝您聯繫我們！你好，謝謝你的來信！
"Dear valued customer, we sincerely apologize."親愛的顧客，我們真誠道歉。親愛的貴賓您好，對於造成您的不便，我們深感抱歉。

A customer letter that says 你 to a stranger reads as rude; a friendly reply that says 您 reads as a robot. Formality is set by the relationship, and only a human knows what that relationship is.

▍7. Broken numbers, dates, and units: small errors, real money

Numbers look like the safest part of a text. They are actually one of the most dangerous, because a wrong number is invisible — it still looks like a number.

OriginalTypical AI outputHuman fix
"1.5 billion users"1.5 億用戶15 億用戶（1 billion = 10 億）
"March 5, 2026"5 號 3 月 2026 年2026 年 3 月 5 日
"The first floor"一樓一樓（美式）／二樓（英式 first floor = 地面上一層）

One wrong digit in a contract, a price, or a deadline is not a style problem — it is a money problem. Every number in a finished translation gets checked against the original, one by one. The same discipline applies to the 5 most common English-to-Chinese mistakes in general.

▍So should you stop using AI translation?

No. Use it for what it is good at: a fast first draft, understanding a foreign text, or translating something nobody will publish. Then add the step AI cannot do — a human reading the result and asking, for every sentence: does this sound like a person wrote it, for this reader, in this situation?
